CASE

PC Case

The Home That Houses It All.

The case is more than just an enclosure - it is an airflow and thermal management system that directly impacts your components' temperatures, noise levels, and lifespan. A poorly ventilated case can reduce cooling performance, even with high-end hardware.

What It Does

The case provides structural housing for all components, determines which motherboard sizes, GPUs, CPU coolers, and radiators can fit, and influences your PC's airflow and appearance. Modern cases are designed with airflow in mind, using mesh panels, optimized fan mounts, and cable management features.

How It Works

Cool air enters through intake fans (typically at the front and/or bottom), while warm air exits through exhaust fans (usually at the rear and top). Positive air pressure (more intake than exhaust) helps reduce dust entering through unfiltered openings, while negative pressure (more exhaust than intake) can remove heat efficiently but may draw in more dust. Many builders prefer slightly positive pressure with dust filters.

Why It Matters for Gaming

A case with poor airflow can increase CPU and GPU temperatures by 10-15C or more compared to a well-ventilated case. Higher temperatures can cause components to reduce boost clocks (thermal throttling), lowering performance. High-performance GPUs especially benefit from good airflow.

Types & Variants

Full Tower

Enthusiast workstations, custom water cooling, high-end builds

Largest common form factor. Supports E-ATX motherboards, large radiators, multiple storage drives, and plenty of expansion space.

Pros:

  • Maximum expansion space
  • Excellent cable management room
  • Supports the largest radiators
  • Excellent airflow potential

Cons:

  • Large footprint
  • Heavy
  • More expensive

Mid Tower

Gaming builds, workstations, general-purpose PCs

The most popular case size. Supports ATX motherboards, large graphics cards, and up to 360mm radiators in many models.

Pros:

  • Excellent balance of size and features
  • Huge variety of models available
  • Suitable for most gaming PCs

Cons:

  • Still fairly large
  • Some budget models have restricted airflow

Compact / SFF (Small Form Factor)

Portable gaming PCs, living room systems, space-saving setups

Small cases designed for Mini-ITX and sometimes Micro-ATX motherboards. Prioritize compact size over expandability.

Pros:

  • Small footprint
  • Portable
  • Clean minimalist appearance

Cons:

  • Limited cooling options
  • Tighter cable management
  • Often more expensive for their size
  • Higher component temperatures if airflow is limited

Key Specifications

Supported Motherboard Size

Critical

Always check which motherboard sizes the case supports (E-ATX, ATX, Micro-ATX, Mini-ITX). Your motherboard must fit inside the case.

Maximum CPU Cooler Height

Critical

Air coolers vary in height. Most mid-tower cases support around 160-170mm coolers, while compact cases often support much shorter coolers.

Maximum GPU Length

Important

Modern graphics cards can be very long. Always check the case's maximum GPU clearance before buying, especially for large triple-fan models.

Radiator Support

Important

Cases list supported radiator sizes such as 120mm, 240mm, 280mm, and 360mm. Always check which mounting positions (front, top, rear, or bottom) support each size.

Airflow Design

Critical

Mesh front panels generally provide much better airflow than solid front panels, allowing cooler air to reach your components more easily.

Drive Bays

Nice to Know

2.5-inch bays are used for SATA SSDs, while 3.5-inch bays are used for HDDs. Most modern gaming PCs require only a few drive bays.

Real-World Gaming Impact

GPU Temperature Under Load

A high-airflow case can keep GPU temperatures significantly lower than a restricted-airflow case, allowing graphics cards to maintain higher boost clocks during gaming.

Lower GPU temperatures and improved sustained performance

System Noise Levels

Good airflow often allows fans to spin more slowly, reducing overall system noise while maintaining safe temperatures.

Quieter gaming experience

Cable Management

Cases with PSU shrouds, cable routing channels, and tie-down points make it easier to hide cables, improving both airflow and appearance.

Cleaner build and improved airflow

Build Accessibility

Features such as tool-free side panels, removable drive cages, and spacious interiors make building, upgrading, and maintaining your PC much easier.

Faster and easier assembly

Common Mistakes

  • Buying a case with a solid front panel that severely restricts airflow.
  • Not checking GPU clearance before purchasing a large graphics card.
  • Ignoring CPU cooler height or radiator compatibility.
  • Forgetting to remove the protective plastic film from tempered glass panels after building.

Fun Facts

  • The Lian Li O11 Dynamic is one of the most popular enthusiast PC cases and has inspired many similar case designs.
  • High-airflow mesh front panels can significantly improve cooling compared to solid-front designs.
  • Most tempered glass PC case side panels are made from heat-treated safety glass, making them much stronger than ordinary glass, although they can still shatter if struck on their edges.
