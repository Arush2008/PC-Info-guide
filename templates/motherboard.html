Motherboard

The Foundation That Connects Everything.

The motherboard is the central hub connecting every component in your PC. Every piece of data - CPU instructions, GPU frames, RAM operations, and storage transfers - flows through pathways etched into the motherboard's PCB.

What it does

The motherboard provides physical sockets and slots for all components, power delivery circuits for the CPU (VRM), high-speed data buses (PCIe lanes), I/O connectivity (USB, audio, networking), and the BIOS/UEFI firmware that initializes everything on boot. It determines which CPUs are compatible, what RAM type is supported, and what features are available.

How it works

The chipset acts as a traffic controller - managing communication between the CPU, PCIe slots, USB ports, and storage devices. The CPU connects via a CPU socket (LGA1700 for Intel 12th-14th Gen, LGA1851 for newer Intel Core Ultra desktop CPUs, AM4 for older AMD Ryzen, and AM5 for Ryzen 7000 and newer). VRM (Voltage Regulator Module) phases convert 12V power from the PSU into the precise voltage the CPU needs, with stronger VRM designs providing cleaner and more stable power delivery for high-performance CPUs.

Why It Matters for Gaming

The wrong motherboard makes your CPU, RAM, and storage incompatible. The chipset determines features such as overclocking support (Z-series/X-series and some newer high-end chipsets), PCIe lanes, M.2 slots, and USB connectivity. Premium motherboards with robust VRMs allow high-end CPUs to maintain boost clocks for longer, improving performance in sustained workloads.

Type & Variants

ATX (Standard)

Gaming builds, high-end workstations

Full-size at 305×244mm. Offers the most PCIe slots, RAM slots, M.2 slots, and expansion options. Best for power users and enthusiasts.

Pros:

  • Most expansion slots
  • More RAM capacity
  • More storage options
  • Usually better VRM designs

Cons:

  • Needs full tower or mid tower case
  • More expensive

mATX (Micro ATX)

Budget and compact gaming builds

Smaller at 244×244mm. Provides most features needed for gaming while fitting into smaller cases.

Pros:

  • Cheaper
  • Fits compact cases
  • Usually supports 2-4 RAM slots
  • Good balance between size and features

Cons:

  • Fewer expansion slots
  • Usually fewer M.2 slots
  • Less room for premium features

ITX (Mini ITX)

Small form factor living room PCs

Tiny at 170×170mm. Usually has one PCIe slot and two RAM slots. Designed for ultra-compact builds.

Pros:

  • Very small form factor
  • Perfect for compact PCs
  • Good for portable builds

Cons:

  • Only 1 PCIe slot
  • Only 2 RAM slots
  • Limited expansion options
  • Pricier per feature

Key Specifications

CPU Socket

Critical

Must match CPU exactly. LGA1700 for Intel 12th-14th Gen, LGA1851 for newer Intel Core Ultra desktop CPUs, AM4 for Ryzen 1000-5000 series, and AM5 for Ryzen 7000 and newer. These are not cross-compatible.

Chipset

Important

Z790/Z890, X670E/X870E = enthusiast chipsets with more features and overclocking support. B760/B650/B850 = mid-range gaming chipsets. H610/A620 = budget chipsets with fewer features. Always verify chipset support before buying.

VRM Phases

Critical

Better VRM designs provide cleaner and more stable power to the CPU. Higher-end CPUs such as Intel Core i9 or AMD Ryzen 9 benefit from stronger VRMs, especially during sustained workloads.

M.2 Slots

Important

Each M.2 slot can hold one NVMe SSD. More slots allow more fast storage. Premium boards can offer multiple M.2 slots with PCIe 4.0 or PCIe 5.0 speeds.

RAM Slots & Max Capacity

Important

ATX boards usually have 4 DIMM slots, while mATX and ITX boards may have fewer. Always check supported DDR generation, maximum capacity, and supported RAM speeds for your kit.

PCIe Version

Important

PCIe 5.0 provides double the bandwidth of PCIe 4.0 per lane. PCIe 5.0 M.2 slots can support next-generation SSDs reaching around 14,000 MB/s, while PCIe 4.0 SSDs typically reach around 7,000 MB/s.

Real-World Gaming Impact

CPU Overclocking

A high-end motherboard with a strong VRM can allow unlocked CPUs to maintain higher boost clocks for longer compared to weaker boards that may reduce performance under heavy loads.

Better sustained CPU performance

NVMe Storage Speed

PCIe 5.0 M.2 slots allow the use of faster Gen 5 SSDs reaching around 14,000 MB/s compared to PCIe 4.0 SSDs around 7,000 MB/s.

2x Maximum sequential storage speed

Multi-Monitor & Audio

The motherboard determines available connectivity such as USB ports, networking, audio hardware, and display outputs (when supported by the CPU).

More connectivity options

Upgrade Flexibility

The right motherboard gives you room to upgrade the CPU, RAM, storage, and expansion cards without replacing the whole system.

Better long-term platform support

Common Mistakes

  • Buying a Z-series board for a non-K CPU when you do not need overclocking features.
  • Getting a DDR4 motherboard for a Ryzen 7000 CPU (AM5 platforms require DDR5).
  • Not checking BIOS compatibility before pairing a newer CPU with an older motherboard.
  • Overspending on the motherboard while using lower-end components.

Fun Facts

  • A modern ATX motherboard can have more than 10 layers of PCB material to improve signal quality and power delivery.
  • High-end motherboard VRMs can deliver hundreds of amps of stable current to modern CPUs.
  • Motherboards do not directly increase gaming FPS significantly, but they determine compatibility, upgrade options, and system stability.
